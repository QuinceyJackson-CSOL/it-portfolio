## Situation
A fitness instructor with a large social following (55,000+ on Instagram) was hosting a 3-hour
Pilates class and seminar. She needed to be heard clearly while moving and teaching, and she
needed music playing under the class. She didn't have a sound system or anyone to run one.

## How I ran the event
1. **Load-in:** delivered and set up the PA and wireless mic system before attendees arrived.
2. **Sound check:** tested the presenter's mic while she moved through the space, and set music
   playback levels so the music sat under her voice instead of fighting it.
3. **Live playback:** ran music for the class portion and switched over cleanly for the seminar.
4. **Troubleshooting:** stayed on site for all 3 hours to handle anything that came up in real time.
5. **Wrap:** tore down and packed out.

## Related work
At a church where I lead A/V, I installed a new 12-channel mixing console and audio equipment
(replacing a 16-channel mixer), connected keyboards and microphones, **labeled every mixer
channel**, and **color-coded and numbered four wireless handheld mics to match their receiver
channels**. Result: volunteers can operate and troubleshoot the system without guessing which
mic is which.

## What I learned
Most live-sound problems are preventable with prep: a real sound check and clear labeling do
more than any fix you try mid-event. It's the same lesson as IT: document it before it breaks.
